FAA Midwest ATC Outage Grounds Flights Across Nine States

What happened: A major telecommunications failure at the Minneapolis Air Route Traffic Control Center on August 6-7 knocked out radar and radio service across 330,000 square miles, grounding and diverting flights across nine Midwest states including Minnesota, Michigan, Kansas, Missouri, and Wisconsin. Delays at MSP averaged 90–205 minutes before systems came back online roughly two hours later.

Why it matters: This is the latest in a pattern of FAA infrastructure failures raising serious questions about the reliability of U.S. air traffic control systems. The FAA has opened a formal investigation into the cause, and the outage re-ignites a national debate about aging ATC equipment and staffing.

The GuyTalk Read: Another day, another FAA meltdown — and this one was a monster. One facility going dark managed to freeze flights across nearly a million square miles of U.S. airspace. If you were trying to fly through Minneapolis or any of the surrounding states Thursday evening, you were stuck. The FAA says telecommunications problems are to blame, but given how often this has happened at major hubs in the past year, 'telecom problem' is starting to feel like a catch-all excuse for a system that's badly overdue for a serious overhaul. Trump is reportedly pushing back on reports of critically depleted air defense stockpiles in the same news cycle — not a great look for the administration's infrastructure narrative.

  • The Minneapolis Air Route Traffic Control Center manages airspace over approximately 330,000 square miles across nine U.S. states.
  • Radar and radio service at the Minneapolis center resumed about two hours after the outage began; the FAA lifted the ground stop at MSP around 4 p.m. Central Time.
  • The FAA attributed the outage to telecommunications problems and has opened a formal investigation into the cause.

What to say: Dude, the whole Midwest airspace just went dark Thursday — one ATC facility failing and nine states of flights get frozen. How is this still happening?

Mahomes Fully Cleared, Looks 'Completely Fine' at Chiefs Camp

What happened
Patrick Mahomes has been fully cleared to participate in training camp after tearing his ACL and LCL in Week 15 last season, with ESPN reporters on-site confirming he's taking all first-team reps and making impressive throws on the surgically repaired knee.
Why it matters
Kansas City went 6-11 without Mahomes last year and lost the AFC West to Denver, making his Week 1 availability against the Broncos on September 14 essentially a must-win. Mahomes is the single biggest storyline of the 2026 NFL season because the entire Chiefs season depends on him being ready.
Big moments
  • Mahomes is rolling out on the surgically repaired knee and making impressive throws at full camp participation
  • Kansas City went 6-11 without him last season, losing the division to Denver—the gap between Mahomes and backup is historically massive
The GuyTalk Read
This is the clearest signal the Chiefs will be ready in Week 1. Andy Reid wouldn't have him taking first-team reps at full-go unless the knee was genuinely healed, and reporters seeing impressive throws on the repaired side removes the biggest question mark of the offseason. Denver won the West last year while Kansas City limped to 6-11, but that divide vanishes the moment Mahomes is back. The Broncos know this too, which is why September 14 feels less like a season opener and more like the actual reset moment.
