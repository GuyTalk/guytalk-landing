Ben Shelton at US Open

Course
Omni Grove Park Inn Resort, Asheville, North Carolina
What happened
Alexander Zverev advanced to the US Open quarterfinals with dominant wins over Botic Van De Zandschulp (6-2 7-5 6-1) and Karen Khachanov (6-3 7-6 7-6), while Ben Shelton beat Frances Tiafoe 4-6 6-3 6-3 7-5 in a longer baseline battle.
Why it matters
Zverev's breadth of opponents—a power baseliner and a shot-making playmaker—shows he can adjust to different styles. Shelton had to gut out a four-set grind against Tiafoe after dropping the first, a reality check that separates pretenders from deep run players.
Big moments
  • Zverev dispatched Khachanov in straight sets despite the tiebreaker pressure, not yielding a set
  • Shelton came back from down a set against Tiafoe and won the fourth set 7-5 in a grinding finish
The GuyTalk Read
Zverev's looking like the most complete player left in his quarter of the draw. He's beating different styles cleanly and not getting dragged into long points he shouldn't win. Shelton showed mettle against a guy who can make him look pedestrian—Tiafoe's game is sharper and more inventive—but he just outlasted him through sheer will and consistency. That matters at a major.
What to know
  • Zverev beat Khachanov 6-3 7-6 7-6 without dropping a set
  • Zverev's first match (Van De Zandschulp) ended 6-2 7-5 6-1
  • Shelton overcame a first-set loss (4-6) and won the match in four sets 6-3 6-3 7-5
What to say
"Shelton had to save his tournament against Tiafoe after dropping the first set—that's the difference between a US Open semifinalist and a second-week exit."

THE GUYTALK READ. MLB

Milwaukee Brewers 20–0 over Cincinnati Reds

What happened
The Milwaukee Brewers obliterated the Cincinnati Reds 20-0, with William Contreras driving in 6 runs on a homer, Brice Turang going 3-for-3 with a home run, and Garrett Mitchell also going deep while collecting 4 RBIs in a complete offensive dismantling.
Why it matters
A 20-0 shutout is baseball rarity—it means the Brewers didn't just outplay Cincinnati, they dominated every inning and never let the other team breathe. The Reds' inability to score a single run against a team's regular rotation is a season-defining collapse.
Big moments
  • William Contreras went 2-for-5 with a solo homer and 6 RBIs, leading the offensive barrage
  • Brice Turang was perfect at the plate (3-for-3) with a home run, and Garrett Mitchell went 3-for-5 with a homer and 4 RBIs, showing the entire Brewers lineup was locked in
The GuyTalk Read
This isn't just a win. This is a statement game where Milwaukee's entire offensive core showed up at once and Cincinnati had no answer. Dustin May threw six innings with six strikeouts and gave up nothing, which means the Brewers' pitching wasn't dominant—they just didn't need it because the bat speed was overwhelming. The Reds got smoked on both sides of the ball, and this kind of complete collapse can rattle a team heading into the final stretch.
What to know
  • Final score: Milwaukee 20, Cincinnati 0
  • William Contreras: 2-5, 1 HR, 6 RBI
  • Brice Turang: 3-3, 1 HR, 4 RBI
  • Garrett Mitchell: 3-5, 1 HR, 4 RBI
  • Dustin May: 6.0 IP, 6 K
What to say
"The Brewers scored 20 runs on just three home runs from Contreras, Turang, and Mitchell—that's not relying on the long ball, that's just every batter being locked in and making hard contact."
Players to know

    What happened: Jon Ronson's new book The Castle chronicles a man who built a Connecticut castle to host a harem of women — and what happened when he invited the wrong guest. Slate has an early look at the book, which digs into the modern manosphere and men 'ripping off their faces.'

    Why it matters: Ronson — the guy who wrote The Men Who Stare at Goats and So You've Been Publicly Shamed — has a track record of finding the most bizarre true stories at the edge of culture and making them feel urgent. This one sounds like it belongs in that tier.

    The GuyTalk Read: Ronson is one of the best writers alive at finding the moment where a subculture tips into something darker, and a Connecticut castle built around control and access is exactly the kind of story he was born to tell. The manosphere angle gives it cultural weight beyond just a weird true-crime yarn — it's a window into how certain ideas about masculinity get taken to their logical extreme. This will be a book people are talking about at bars by October.

    What to say: Jon Ronson wrote a book about a guy who built a castle in Connecticut to run a harem — and somehow it's also about the manosphere. That's the fall read.
